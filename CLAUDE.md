# CLAUDE.md

Ben's Polyphonic Pitch Shifter (PolyPitch in code, file names and `polypitch~`): a polyphonic pitch shifter for
live playing. One C++ engine, offered as a plug-in (AU, VST3), a Max
object (`polypitch~`) and a Max for Live device. Open source (MIT). Ben is the only maintainer; he directs agents
and judges by behavior and by ear, not by code.

## The shape of the repo

- `engine/PolyPitchEngine.h`: the shifter. One header, no dependencies, one sample in and one out.
- `engine/PolyPitchProcessor.h`: mix and tone around it. The plug-in and the Max object both call this and nothing else.
- `reference/polypitch_ref.py`: the same algorithm in Python. **The engine is a line-for-line port of it.** Ideas
  are tried in the reference first, then ported; a change to one without the other is a bug.
- `engine/PolyPitchFilters.h`: generated by `reference/make_filters.py`. Never edit it by hand.
- `plugin/`, `max/`: thin wrappers. `max/device/Ben's Polyphonic Pitch Shifter.amxd` and the help patch are generated by
  `max/tools/maxpatch.py`.
- `tests/`: synthetic signals with exact answers, the scorecard, engine against reference.
- `docs/how-it-works.md` first, then `docs/design-notes.md` (what was tried and why it failed) before changing the
  engine. `docs/benchmarks.md` holds every number the README quotes. `docs/controls.md` says what each control does
  to the sound; when a control changes, it changes in the same commit.

## From edit to main

1. `scripts/gate.sh` (about six minutes): builds the tools, input samples that are not numbers counted as silence,
   engine against reference at 44.1 and 48 kHz, scorecard at -12, +2 and +12 (and at +12 in the balanced and clean
   responses, and all three with the quality set to lite and to eco) inside its limits. It must pass before a commit.
2. If the sound changed, run `python tests/scorecard.py engine all` and put the before and after lines in the
   commit message. If a number in `docs/benchmarks.md` or the README moved, update it in the same commit.
3. Commit to `main`. Push only when Ben asks.
4. Tell Ben what behaves differently and what was not run.

## Rules

- Measure, then claim. Say what was run and what was not. The Max object and device cannot be tested without
  launching Max; say so rather than implying they were.
- **Do not start Max's audio (the check patch) while Live or another audio program is running**: it can switch the
  interface's sample rate under them. Never quit Max or change its audio settings.
- No audio that is not Ben's to give: no sample-library content, no commercial presets, no recordings from his
  projects. Tests run on synthetic signals. Real-material numbers in the docs come from material kept outside the
  repo.
- The reference device named in `docs/benchmarks.md` was only ever measured as a black box (audio in, audio out).
  Keep it that way: no decompiling, no disassembly.
- The engine stays standard C++17 with no dependencies. No allocation where audio is processed.
- Explain by behavior, not by code. American spelling.
- Users see "Ben's Polyphonic Pitch Shifter" (plug-in name, device file and title, Max package, docs). "PolyPitch"
  stays only where users do not see it; Line 6 uses "Poly Pitch" for a Helix effect.

## Things that are easy to get wrong

- A partial lives in two neighboring bands. Anything that treats a band on its own must keep the two shares in
  step, or the partial comes out quiet (see "followers" and "placement" in `docs/how-it-works.md`).
- Shifting up and shifting down use different settings on purpose (`setSemitones` in the engine, the `None`
  defaults in the reference). A change that helps one direction often hurts the other; score both.
- Shifting up by less than a fifth has settings of its own (a band leaves a reader sooner, and more beats have to
  hold before a reader takes them: `setSemitones`). The octave rows do not see them: score +2 and +5 as well.
- Shifting up has three responses (`setResponse`: fast, balanced, clean; different band filters, and clean has
  bands half as wide). Fast is the default and what a number means when it does not say. A change to the up-shift
  logic moves all three: `python tests/scorecard.py engine balanced 2 7 12`, and the same with `clean`.
- Quality has three settings (`setQuality`: full, lite, eco). Lite looks for beating only below 2.5 kHz and,
  shifting up on fast and balanced, leaves out every second band above 1.25 kHz: the bands are then numbered
  without the gaps, and a band's neighbor can be one or two of the bank's bands away (`kOf`, `kGap`, `w2` in the
  engine; `kof`, `wg` in the reference). Eco is lite with 64-sample frames: the frame length `H` is a member of the
  engine, not a constant (`frameSettings`; `* rH` and `& hMask` in the hot paths, exact because it is a power of
  two), and everything counted in frames (lags, the window, the smoothing) follows it. A change to anything that
  looks at a neighboring band or counts frames has to work for all three: `python tests/scorecard.py engine lite`
  and `engine eco`, and the gate runs engine against reference in both.
- The scorecard averages. Held chords after the attack and pitch that moves have their own tables in
  `docs/benchmarks.md` because the average hid them.
- numba: unifying a `prange` index with an int32 array element gives float64. Cast to `np.int64` before indexing.
- `auval -a` scans every Audio Unit and takes many minutes. Validate one: `auval -v aufx PlyP Bjuo`.
- CPU numbers move with whatever else the computer is doing. Compare before and after in the same sitting, runs
  interleaved, and take the lowest of several; trust the 99.9th-percentile block of `polypitch_profile`, not the
  single heaviest one.
